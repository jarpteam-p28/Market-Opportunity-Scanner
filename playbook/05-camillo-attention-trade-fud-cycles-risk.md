# Notes: Chris Camillo — FUD cycles, the attention trade, distribution moats and risk rules (summary in our words)
Source: Ice Coffee Hour interview Pablo shared (after Camillo's ~40% drawdown and his Amazon earnings trade). These are opinions. He describes his own trading account as ultra-high-risk; it is not a template.

## What happened
- **The drawdown.** His account fell about 40% in a couple of weeks, mainly through Bloom Energy and other AI names.
- **Why he says it fell.** Forced selling: a large AI-focused fund being margin-called, plus leveraged South Korean investors who held the same names. Narratives added to it (Chinese open-source models like Kimi, worries about AI capex).
- **His read.** Forced selling ends and then reverses. The hard part is the timing.
- **The Amazon trade.** He made it back on Amazon earnings. His thesis was about how management would handle the capex question on the call, not only the numbers. He put it at roughly 80/20 and did 100+ hours of research.

## Ideas we can use
1. **The attention trade / FUD cycles.**
   - Markets can be moved for a while by influential voices and by narratives most people don't understand (AI).
   - He expects repeated AI "fear" cycles and wants to measure how fast a bearish narrative is ACCELERATING, so he isn't caught again.
   - He also wants to know where the leverage sits (leveraged funds, Korea, leveraged ETFs), because leverage makes moves fast in both directions.
2. **Go to the ground truth.** To learn whether AI really pays off, read the people doing the work: developers and IT staff on Reddit, X, forums and YouTube talking about whether AI tools save money at their companies. Do this before it shows up in earnings or the financial press. Skip investor opinion and noise.
3. **The first hard evidence of the "AI efficiency wave."** One example: a booking company's earnings report said AI tools cut its customer-service costs. When more earnings reports say "AI lowered our costs", he expects the next big AI leg up to broaden beyond the ~20 mega caps.
4. **Distribution moats win.** If intelligence becomes cheap and available to everyone, the winners are companies that already own distribution (logistics, shelf space, customers). Example: Amazon. They get to make things cheaper and better first.
5. **On the bears (Eisman going to cash, Burry):** his view is that nobody, bull or bear, really knows how AI plays out. Run scenarios and update them daily instead of following gurus.

## Risk rules he states
- **Separate accounts.** Keep a designated high-risk account apart from retirement and college money.
- **Size by conviction.** Low or medium conviction gets a tiny position, about 2–3% of a full-conviction size. You still get the "action" without the damage.
- **When unsure, sell half.**
- **Don't marry a stock.** If evidence breaks the thesis, exit, and consider the other side.
- **Study the other side** of the trade more than your own thesis.
- **Plan for what you know you don't know** (politics, war, rumours) and price that into the odds.
- **Stay liquid** in fast-changing times; he stopped investing in private companies.
- **A thesis can be overtaken by other news.** Sweetgreen's wrap story became irrelevant once a lettuce-related illness story hit restaurant salad chains, and Cava turned out to be the stronger competitor.

## How the scanner uses this
- **AI fear/narrative meter.** Counts bearish AI narratives ("AI bubble", capex fears, open-source/China models, circular financing, margin calls) in Reddit and headlines vs their baseline, and flags acceleration.
- **AI efficiency evidence meter.** Counts posts and headlines saying AI cut costs, headcount or customer-service spend.
- **Ground-truth subreddits** (developers, IT, ML practitioners) are added to the Reddit scan.
- **Distribution moats theme basket.**
- **Synthesizer sizing.** Each watch-list idea carries a conviction level. Low or medium conviction means a starter position only.
