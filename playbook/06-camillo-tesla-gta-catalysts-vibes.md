# Notes: Chris Camillo — Tesla/Optimus doubts, buying dips for the right reasons, the GTA 6 catalyst, "vibe" markets (summary in our words)
Source: Ice Coffee Hour short episode Pablo shared. These are his opinions and his own positions.

## Tesla
- **His only reason to own Tesla was the Optimus humanoid robot.** By his account, the program has struggled: talent leaving, internal friction, the robot hands, and a public update that never came. He says it isn't ready to scale.
- **Self-driving and robotaxi:** he thinks these are mostly priced in, and competition is now real (Waymo, Chinese makers).
- **Lesson for us:** a stock bought for one story is only as good as the evidence on that story. Check progress, don't assume it.

## Apple
- He sees no big under-appreciated growth driver right now.
- He thinks Apple missed a natural edge in home robots: consumer trust, a closed ecosystem, privacy, hardware skill.

## Amazon: add on dips only when the reason doesn't matter
- After strong earnings the stock drifted back down. He says that's because Amazon doesn't excite "dopamine" investors.
- **His rule:**
  - If a drop comes from something he judges immaterial long term (a lawsuit headline, a half-point move in rates), he adds.
  - If the reason is material, he reassesses and may trim.
  - Classify WHY it fell before acting.

## GTA 6 / Take-Two (an event catalyst)
- **His scenario approach.** Know the numbers the market expects: first-year units of 30–50 million, price per copy, and online spending per monthly user, from about $20 to about $60. Judge which scenario is likely from evidence. He read ~30,000 organic comments on the gameplay trailer and judged depth of interest very high.
- **Upside scenario:** online micro-transactions of roughly $2–3 billion a year if engagement is deep. GTA 5 still earns hundreds of millions a year, 13 years later.
- **Noise vs signal.** He discounts a Sensor Tower pre-sales estimate as low-quality: it's the firm's first try on a console game, and digital games don't need pre-orders. He also says a delay past the holiday season would hurt badly.
- **Second-order trades:**
  - Food delivery (DoorDash, Uber Eats) as millions of players stay home.
  - Possible pressure on movie and TV viewing.
  - Whatever else changes when a huge audience spends weeks inside one game.
- **"Cultural moments":** people crave shared experiences (launch lines, limited releases, mass gaming events), partly from loneliness. Those moments can be bigger than expected.

## "Vibe" markets
- Many investors now trade on vibes, not benchmarks. Work that is right can take longer to show up in the price, because few people do it.
- Ask "what exactly is priced in?" in numbers. If nobody can say, the stock can swing either way on the event.
- Meme spikes (GoPro): he takes only a tiny position to cure FOMO, if he takes any.
- **Build-A-Bear:** a small company "one hit product away"; collaborations and limited releases on the Crocs playbook could move it.
