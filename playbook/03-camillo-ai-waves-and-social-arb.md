# Notes: Chris Camillo on the AI super cycle and social arbitrage (summary in our words)
Source: YouTube interview Pablo shared (Oct 2026). These are the speaker's opinions and trades; he says himself that his use of leverage should not be copied.

## Three waves of the AI super cycle (his framework)
1. **The "wow" moment:** AI can think.
2. **Infrastructure:** chips, data centers, energy, memory, rare earths. He's still invested across this.
3. **The "AI efficiency wave":** companies USING AI to cut big costs (customer service, admin, logistics, repetitive white-collar work), so margins rise.
   - He calls this the last easy trade of the cycle and expects it within ~1–2 years.
   - He expects it to be broad-based, not just 15–20 mega caps.
   - His top pick for it is Amazon: AWS, its own AI chip (Trainium), AI in retail logistics, and AI in advertising.

## How he trades: social / attention arbitrage
- Buy when you spot information the market under-appreciates. Exit at "information parity", when the world sees what you saw.
- He ignores price targets, technicals and most macro noise.
- The signal is a CHANGE in behaviour or attention, visible in Google Trends, TikTok, Reddit and in-store checks.
- **Examples he gave:**
  - **Coconut water (Vita Coco):** a missed trade. Google Trends showed a 3–4 month "hockey stick" before a big earnings jump.
  - **Sweetgreen:** a new product (wraps) riding an existing trend (chicken Caesar wraps), with an influencer push, store-level sales checks and high short interest (~23%).
  - **Swatch × AP:** a collaboration hype event. The move came on the announcement and was mostly done by launch day.
- **His rules:**
  - Ride an existing trend rather than bet on a company inventing one.
  - Favour new products that change how a beaten-down company is seen.
  - A small company with a hit product moves more than a giant.
  - Never sell into a move you caused yourself.
- **Conviction:** deep research before entry (he says 50–100 hours). If the price falls but the thesis is unchanged, conviction should go up, not down.

## Other points
- He thinks higher rates help: they punish weak companies and reward companies whose AI use raises returns on capital.
- **Biggest risk he sees:** layoffs from AI efficiency hitting faster than new jobs appear, causing a recession.
- **Collectibles:** an asset becomes investable when it becomes gradable or standardised (manga). Nostalgia cycles shift by decade.

## How the scanner uses this
- **"Rising tickers":** Reddit tickers whose mentions jumped vs their own baseline, not just the most-mentioned ones.
- **Consumer social-arb watch list (config `consumer_watch`):** Google Trends terms for products and brands, flagged on hockey-stick rises. Add terms as you spot them.
- **A theme basket for the "AI efficiency wave"** (labor-heavy companies that could gain margins), tracked against the infrastructure themes so we can see if or when money rotates there.
- The journal scores each idea against the S&P. "Information parity" is the exit idea to test.
